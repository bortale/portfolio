My Role

I identified a critical performance gap that could not be solved through hiring or volume alone. As the person responsible for the agency's operations and outcomes, I designed and implemented the entire learning and performance system from scratch — needs analysis, onboarding architecture, competency mapping, continuous learning infrastructure, and a peer mentoring pipeline.

This project clarified something I had intuited for years: performance problems are almost always system design problems — and L&D is the lever that makes everything else work.

01 — Problem

The business was growing. Performance wasn't.

02 — Initial Failure & Insight

Training alone didn't work

The first intervention — adding more training — failed. Quality issues persisted, the team became overwhelmed, and workload increased without any improvement in output.

💡 Key insight: Training without system design does not improve performance. The root cause was not a knowledge gap — it was the absence of entry filtering, structured onboarding, capability alignment, and a defined learning pathway.

This failure reframed the question. Instead of asking "what do people need to learn?" I started asking "what system needs to exist for people to perform?"

03 — Solution

A five-component capability system

I designed and implemented a learning and performance system that integrated hiring, onboarding, workflow, and culture into a single coherent structure.

1

Entry Filtering & Assessment

Theoretical interview + written translation test. Only candidates with baseline capability entered the system — eliminating downstream rework before it started.

2

Structured 3-Week Onboarding

Weeks 1–2: Guided learning — onboarding framework, 10-minute micro-tests (grammar, phrasing, decision-making), simple tasks, assigned mentor.
Week 3: Controlled autonomy — self-selected assignments, increasing complexity, deadline responsibility, structured feedback.

3

Competency Mapping

Translator level defined within 2 weeks. Specialisation identified across three tracks: business/analytical, crypto & market, literary/creative. Enabled task–capability alignment and eliminated "taking tasks beyond skill level."

4

Embedded Continuous Learning

Bi-monthly expert sessions, weekly roundtables for error analysis and best practices, ongoing micro-testing. Learning embedded in workflow — not separate from it.

5

Peer Learning & Growth Pipeline

Mentor–mentee pairs, responsibility for knowledge transfer, high performers growing into mentor → manager roles. Result: 2 internal managers promoted from within the system.

05 — Reflection

What I learned — and what I'd do differently

"Performance problems are rarely caused by lack of effort. They're caused by the absence of structured learning, capability alignment, and workflow design. Once the system existed, behaviour changed — not because people were told to change, but because the environment made the right behaviour the default."

If I were to replicate this system in a new organisation tomorrow, I would start with the same diagnostic sequence: map the gap between task demands and current capability, define the minimum viable entry bar, build a short structured onboarding with embedded checkpoints, then layer in continuous learning and peer support.

What I would do differently: involve senior team members earlier in the design process to reduce initial resistance, and define success metrics before launch rather than measuring retrospectively.

06 — Decision-Making

How I think as an L&D designer

→

I start with symptoms, not assumptions

Rework, slow onboarding, manager overload — these are signals, not the problem. I resist jumping to solutions before understanding what's actually breaking.

→

I test hypotheses before scaling

The first intervention failed. I treated that as data, not defeat — and redesigned from the root cause upward.

→

I observe behaviour, not just knowledge

The question wasn't "do they know it?" — it was "how do they choose tasks, manage deadlines, and ask for help when they're stuck?"

→

I embed learning into the work itself

Micro-tests, peer feedback, and error analysis became part of the weekly workflow — not separate events people attend and forget.

→

I use culture as a reinforcement layer

Recognition, visible progress, and mentorship roles converted resistant senior staff into active knowledge sharers — because the system gave them a meaningful role.
